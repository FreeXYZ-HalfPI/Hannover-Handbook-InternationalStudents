## 1. Purpose

How your spouse or partner and children can join you in Germany (Familiennachzug), what is required, and where to find support for studying with children.

## 2. Who needs this?

- 🌍 **Non-EU students** who are married or have children and want their family with them.
- 🌍 **EU/EEA citizens:** family members (including non-EU family members) have free-movement rights; non-EU family members may need an entry visa, which is issued free of charge and quickly ([Make it in Germany – Family reunification](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/visa-residence/family-reunification)).

## 3. Steps

1.  **General requirements for a non-EU spouse:** usually adequate health insurance, sufficient money to support the family and enough living space; both spouses must be at least 18 ([Make it in Germany – Spouses joining non-EU citizens](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/visa-recognition/family-reunification/spouses-joining-non-eu-citizens)).
2.  **German language for the spouse:** as a basic rule, the law requires the spouse to communicate in German at a basic level (A1) ([§ 30 (1) AufenthG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/aufenthg_2004/__30.html)). There are exceptions — e.g. for spouses of citizens of the visa-free countries in § 41 AufenthV and for spouses of skilled workers ([Make it in Germany](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/visa-recognition/family-reunification/spouses-joining-non-eu-citizens)). ⚠️ The law has no specific exception for spouses of **students**; ask the German mission whether A1 is required in your case.
3.  **Financial proof for the family:** expect to show more money than the single-student amount (rent plus the family's living costs). ⚠️ Ask the mission for the amount it applies.
4.  **Children:** requirements depend on the child's age and the family situation ([Make it in Germany – Family reunification](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/visa-recognition/family-reunification)).
5.  **Visa application:** family members apply at the German mission responsible for their place of residence. 🌍 In some countries missions have long waiting lists or limited capacity for family reunification, or work through partners such as IOM; check your mission's website early ([Federal Foreign Office – German missions abroad](https://www.auswaertiges-amt.de/de/ReiseUndSicherheit/deutsche-auslandsvertretungen/03-webseitenav)).
6.  **Documents from your country** (marriage and birth certificates) may need an apostille or legalisation and a certified translation. 🌍 Which one depends on the issuing country — ask the German mission in that country ([Federal Foreign Office – German missions abroad](https://www.auswaertiges-amt.de/de/ReiseUndSicherheit/deutsche-auslandsvertretungen/03-webseitenav)). ⚠️
7.  **After arrival:** family members must register their address and get a residence permit before their visa expires; book the Ausländerbehörde appointment early ([Make it in Germany](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/visa-recognition/family-reunification/spouses-joining-non-eu-citizens)). → [5. Registering your address](05-anmeldung.md), [10. Residence permit](10-residence-permit.md)
8.  **Work for spouses:** spouses who join a student generally may work. ⚠️ Check the conditions noted on their residence permit.
9.  **Childcare and school:** children aged 1+ have a legal right to a childcare place; school is compulsory from about age 6 ([Make it in Germany – Childcare](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/living-in-germany/family-reunification/child-care); [Make it in Germany – School system](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/en/living-in-germany/family-reunification/school-system)). Child benefit (Kindergeld) may be available depending on your status ([Federal Employment Agency – Child benefit](https://www.arbeitsagentur.de/en/familie-und-kinder/kindergeld)).
10. **Studying with children at LUH:** the family service of the Office for Equal Opportunities and Diversity advises students with children ([LUH – Studying with a child](https://www.chancenvielfalt.uni-hannover.de/familienservice/studieren-mit-kind)). → [27. ChancenVielfalt](../03-university-services/27-chancenvielfalt.md)
11. **Inviting family for a short visit (Schengen visitor visa):** a host living in Hannover can sign a **formal obligation letter (Verpflichtungserklärung)** covering the guest's living costs, including medical treatment. Conditions: you live in Hannover, are at least 18, hold a residence permit valid for the whole visit, have sufficient income (last three payslips) and receive no social benefits. The fee is **€29** (also if your income turns out to be insufficient), processing takes about four to five weeks, and the obligation applies for **five years** from the guest's entry. Apply online or by appointment at the Ausländerbehörde ([City of Hannover – Verpflichtungserklärung](https://hannover.gov.de/buergerservice/dienstleistungen/verpflichtungserklaerung-abgeben-1322-0.html?myMedium=1)). ⚠️ If the guest uses public funds, the host must pay them back.

## 5. Responsible office

- **Visa:** the German mission responsible for your family's place of residence
- **Residence permit after arrival:** Ausländerbehörde → [10. Residence permit](10-residence-permit.md)
- **Family service at LUH:** Hochschulbüro für ChancenVielfalt — [chancenvielfalt.uni-hannover.de](https://www.chancenvielfalt.uni-hannover.de/)
