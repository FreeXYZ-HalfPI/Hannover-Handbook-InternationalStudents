# 6. Opening a Bank Account — Sparkasse and Other Banks

> [!TIP]
> **Last reviewed:** 2026-10-26 · **Section:** [Section 1 — Arriving in Hannover & First Steps](README.md) · **Key:** ⚠️ = needs confirmation · ℹ️ = note · 🌍 = depends on your nationality

## 1. Purpose

Regular payments in Germany — semester fee, rent, phone bills — are usually made by bank transfer or direct debit, so a German current account (Girokonto) is almost essential ([Studentenwerk Hannover – First steps](https://www.studentenwerk-hannover.de/en/international-students/first-steps-in-germany)). The monthly payout from a blocked account is also paid into this account.

## 2. Who needs this?

- **All** students, especially non-EU students with a blocked account.
- 🌍 EU students can legally use an account from any SEPA country (IBAN discrimination is not allowed), but a German account is often still more practical ([European Commission – Your Europe: Bank accounts](https://europa.eu/youreurope/citizens/consumers/financial-products-and-services/bank-accounts-eu/index_en.htm)).

## 3. Steps

1.  **Register your address first** — the registration certificate is needed to open an account ([Studentenwerk](https://www.studentenwerk-hannover.de/en/international-students/first-steps-in-germany)). → [5. Registering your address](05-anmeldung.md)
2.  According to the university, some banks also ask for your **tax ID (Steuer-ID)** ([LUH – Entry and Residence](https://www.uni-hannover.de/en/universitaet/internationales/wege-nach-hannover/einreise-aufenthalt)). ℹ️ Each bank's list of documents differs; ask before you go.
3.  **Right to a basic account:** everyone legally resident in the EU — including people without a fixed address, asylum seekers and people with a tolerated stay — has the right to a **basic payment account (Basiskonto)** ([Payment Accounts Act § 31 ZKG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/zkg/__31.html); [BaFin](https://www.bafin.de/EN/Homepage/homepage_node.html)). The bank must offer you the contract **within ten business days** of receiving your complete application. Apply in writing and keep a receipt; if a bank refuses you, ask for the refusal in writing and contact BaFin. ℹ️ Students often found that one branch refused while another branch or bank accepted them.
4.  Many banks and Sparkassen offer **free accounts for students**. If the account is free only for students, show an **enrolment certificate** — and a new one **every semester** ([Studentenwerk](https://www.studentenwerk-hannover.de/en/international-students/first-steps-in-germany)).
5.  **Online banks** can be opened by video identification and often work in English. ℹ️ Check that your passport/nationality is accepted for video-ident and that the bank issues a German IBAN (DE…).
6.  A debit card (**Girocard**, also called EC card) is useful — some shops and canteens accept only Girocard or cash ([Studentenwerk](https://www.studentenwerk-hannover.de/en/international-students/first-steps-in-germany)).
7.  Pay the semester fee from a German account; international transfers cost fees and are slow ([Studentenwerk](https://www.studentenwerk-hannover.de/en/international-students/first-steps-in-germany)). → [13. Semester fee and LeibnizCard](../02-starting-your-studies/13-semester-fee-leibnizcard.md)
8.  🌍 **Sanctioned countries:** some banks run extra checks for nationals or residents of countries under EU sanctions. → [62. Sanctions and payment restrictions](../08-by-nationality/62-sanctions-payment-restrictions.md)
9.  **Blocked account for extending your residence permit:** use the amount and form that the Ausländerbehörde's own checklist asks for, not what others tell you. → [10. Residence permit](10-residence-permit.md)
10. **Cash:** if you bring €10,000 or more in cash from outside the EU, declare it to customs and keep the receipt; banks usually ask where large cash deposits come from ([Zoll – Cash](https://www.zoll.de/EN/Private-individuals/Travel/Entering-Germany/Restrictions/Cash/cash_node.html)). → [3. Arriving in Hannover](03-arrival-transport.md)

## 7. Student experiences (unofficial)

> [!NOTE]
> This section is for personal experiences; it is not a general rule or legal advice. Share yours (with a date) on [page 57](../07-resources/57-contribute-experiences.md) or by opening a GitHub issue.

- **Branches differ a lot:** some opened an account the same day; others asked for the enrolment certificate or Steuer-ID, or sent the documents to head office for review. Several students who were refused opened an account at another branch or bank. ℹ️ This handbook does not recommend any bank.
- **Fees:** monthly account fees and card fees at the same bank were reported differently depending on age and account type — ask for the price list (Preis- und Leistungsverzeichnis) before signing.
- **Moving to another city:** Sparkassen are local; students usually opened a new account in the new city and closed the old one.
- **Online banking from abroad:** some German banking apps did not work from certain countries without a VPN; test access before travelling home.
