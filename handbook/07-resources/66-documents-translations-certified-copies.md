## 1. Purpose

Questions about **renewing a passport**, **certified translations** and **certified copies** come up again and again. This page explains what is done in Germany, what can only be done in your home country, and where to go.

## 2. Who needs this?

- Students whose passport is about to expire or must be replaced.
- Anyone who needs a translation or certified copy for the university, the Ausländerbehörde, an insurer, an employer or the driving-licence office.

## 3. Steps

### A) Your passport

1.  **Check your passport's validity early.** A German residence permit is usually issued at most until your passport expires, so renew or replace your passport **before** applying to extend your residence permit. → [10. Residence permit](../01-arriving-in-hannover/10-residence-permit.md) ⚠️ Based on student experience; ask the Ausländerbehörde.
2.  **Your legal duties:** apply for a new passport **in good time before it expires**, and report a lost passport to the Ausländerbehörde **without delay** ([§ 56 AufenthV](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/aufenthv/__56.html)).
3.  🌍 **Where:** only **your own country's embassy or consulate** can issue a passport. Find it in the Federal Foreign Office's list ([Federal Foreign Office – Missions of other states in Germany](https://www.auswaertiges-amt.de/de/reiseundsicherheit/vertretungen-anderer-staaten)). Many countries require an online application first and an in-person visit for identification; consulates outside Berlin may offer only some services — check before you travel.
4.  🌍 **Military service, deposits or study obligations** in your home country are decided only by your country's authorities and embassy; ask them before changing anything (e.g. your place of residence in your passport, or travelling home).
5.  **After you get a new passport:** your passport number is linked to your residence card. Tell the Ausländerbehörde about the new passport and, until your card is updated, **carry the old passport as well**, especially when travelling. ℹ️ Student experience.

### B) Documents from your home country: translation, legalisation and evaluation

1.  **Certified translation in Germany:** German offices usually accept translations by a **sworn translator (beeidigte/ermächtigte Übersetzer)**. Search the official joint database of the German state courts by language ([justiz-dolmetscher.de](https://www.justiz-dolmetscher.de/)).
2.  🌍 **Apostille or legalisation:** whether a foreign document needs an apostille or legalisation depends on the issuing country, and for many countries it can only be done **there**. Ask the German mission in that country ([Federal Foreign Office – German missions abroad](https://www.auswaertiges-amt.de/de/ReiseUndSicherheit/deutsche-auslandsvertretungen/03-webseitenav)) and ask the German office or university whether it needs it — before you leave home.
3.  **Evaluation of a foreign degree:** for work or further studies, a statement of comparability (Zeugnisbewertung) is issued by the **ZAB**. You apply online with a BundID account; processing takes about three months once all documents are in ([ZAB – Statement of comparability](https://zab.kmk.org/de/zeugnisbewertung/antrag)). → [1. Applying](../01-arriving-in-hannover/01-application-uni-assist.md), [46. After graduation](../05-work-finance-career/46-after-graduation.md)
4.  **AI translation** is fine for understanding a document, but it is **not a certified translation**. If an office asks for a certified one, you can send an unofficial translation for now and say that the certified one is on its way. ℹ️ Student experience.

### C) Certified copies (Beglaubigung) in Hannover

1.  **At the university:** the LUH certification office (Beglaubigungsstelle) certifies **free of charge** documents issued by LUH or needed for use at LUH; Monday–Friday 09:00–12:00, **by appointment only**. Bring the original and a copy; you usually get it back on the spot ([LUH – Certification office](https://www.uni-hannover.de/universitaet/campus-stadt/beglaubigungsstelle)).
2.  **For other purposes:** a Bürgeramt or a notary (Notar), for a fee. ℹ️ Some universities and offices accept only notarised copies — ask the receiving office.

## 5. Responsible office

- **Passport:** your country's embassy or consulate ([Federal Foreign Office – Missions of other states in Germany](https://www.auswaertiges-amt.de/de/reiseundsicherheit/vertretungen-anderer-staaten))
- **Certified copies at the university:** Beglaubigungsstelle der Leibniz Universität Hannover, building 1101, room A112 · beglaubigungsstelle@zuv.uni-hannover.de ([LUH](https://www.uni-hannover.de/universitaet/campus-stadt/beglaubigungsstelle))
- **Sworn translators:** [justiz-dolmetscher.de](https://www.justiz-dolmetscher.de/) — joint database of the German state courts
- **Degree evaluation:** Zentralstelle für ausländisches Bildungswesen (ZAB) ([ZAB](https://zab.kmk.org/de/zeugnisbewertung/antrag))

## 7. Student experiences (unofficial)

> [!NOTE]
> This section is for personal experiences; it is not a general rule or legal advice. Share yours (with a date) on [page 57](57-contribute-experiences.md) or by opening a GitHub issue.

- **Embassy visits (2025–2026):** several students were seen without an online appointment but had to arrive **very early** and wait until the afternoon. Phones and bags were often handed in at the entrance. Bring copies of everything you uploaded online.
- **Replacing a passport early:** some embassies only replaced passports with less than six months' validity left; others accepted a change of residence as a reason. ⚠️
- **Delivery:** new passports were usually sent by post a few weeks later.
- **Privacy:** never post scans of your passport or your file numbers in public groups.
