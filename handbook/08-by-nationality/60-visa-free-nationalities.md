## 1. Purpose

Citizens of some countries can enter Germany without a visa and apply for their study residence permit after arrival. This saves the embassy step — but there are traps, especially around working before the permit is issued.

## 2. Who needs this?

- **Study and work, visa-free (§ 41 (1) AufenthV):** Australia, Canada, Israel, Japan, New Zealand, Republic of Korea (South Korea), United Kingdom, United States ([§ 41 AufenthV](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/aufenthv/__41.html)).
- **Study only, visa-free (§ 41 (2) AufenthV):** Andorra, Brazil, El Salvador, Honduras, Monaco, San Marino — only if you do not want to work before getting the permit ([§ 41 AufenthV](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/aufenthv/__41.html)).
- 🌍 Citizens of other visa-free countries (e.g. many Latin American and some Asian countries) may only visit for **90 days as tourists** — for studies you need a national visa → [61. Visa-required countries](61-visa-required-countries-aps.md).

## 3. Steps

1.  **Entry:** enter with your passport. Carry your admission letter, proof of finances and insurance in case border police ask about the purpose of your stay. ℹ️
2.  **Register your address** within two weeks. → [5. Registering your address](../01-arriving-in-hannover/05-anmeldung.md)
3.  **Apply for the residence permit within 90 days of arrival** at the Ausländerbehörde (online portal in the city of Hannover) ([LUH – Entry and Residence](https://www.uni-hannover.de/en/universitaet/internationales/wege-nach-hannover/einreise-aufenthalt)). → [10. Residence permit](../01-arriving-in-hannover/10-residence-permit.md) ℹ️ Don't wait until day 89 — appointments take time; your application keeps you lawfully in Germany.
4.  **Proof of finances:** like other non-EU students you must show about €992/month (2026), e.g. via a blocked account, scholarship or Verpflichtungserklärung ([German missions in Italy](https://italien.diplo.de/it-de/service/visa-aktuell/2586756-2586756)). You can open a blocked account after arrival, but doing it beforehand speeds things up.
5.  **Working:** you may **not** work until you hold a residence permit that allows it. Group C citizens with a national visa may work in the first 90 days; if you want to start a job immediately, the university recommends getting a visa beforehand ([LUH – Entry and Residence](https://www.uni-hannover.de/en/universitaet/internationales/wege-nach-hannover/einreise-aufenthalt)). Group D citizens who plan to work must get a visa in their home country before arriving ([LUH – Entry and Residence](https://www.uni-hannover.de/en/universitaet/internationales/wege-nach-hannover/einreise-aufenthalt)).
6.  **Health insurance:** statutory student insurance after enrolment, or travel insurance until then. 🌍 US, Canadian, Israeli, Japanese, Korean and Australian citizens: some home policies are not accepted — check the § 11 SGB V requirements. ⚠️ → [7. Health insurance](../01-arriving-in-hannover/07-health-insurance.md)
7.  **Driving licence:** many of these countries/states are on the list for conversion without tests (Annex 11 FeV). → [38. Driving licence](../04-everyday-life/38-driving-licence.md)
8.  **After graduation:** citizens of § 41 (1) countries have easier access to the labour market. → [46. After graduation](../05-work-finance-career/46-after-graduation.md)
9.  🌍 **UK citizens:** since Brexit, UK citizens are third-country nationals but listed in § 41 (1) AufenthV. UK citizens who lived in Germany before 2021 have special Withdrawal Agreement rights ⚠️ Ask the Ausländerbehörde if this applies to you.
